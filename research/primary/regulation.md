# Regulatory and standards sources: primary verification (checked 15 Nov 2026)

Labels: V = official text read; R = secondary source or search excerpt; U = not found.
Method note: several official hosts (EUR-Lex, media.defense.gov, bis.org PDFs through the fetch tool) block automated clients. Where noted, the official file was obtained by another official route (Publications Office Cellar, BIS publications path, EC newsroom redirect, NIST nvlpubs). ## 1. CPMI-IOSCO, Principles for financial market infrastructures (PFMI) -- V
- URL read: https://www.bis.org/publications/principles-financial-market-infrastructures.pdf (landing page https://www.bis.org/cpmi/publ/d101.htm; the shorthand d101a.pdf only serves the landing page to the fetch tool).
- Date/version: published 16 April 2012, final, CPSS Papers 101 (188 pp.). Disclosure framework and assessment methodology followed in December 2012.
- Principle 2 (Governance): KC2 requires documented governance arrangements with "clear and direct lines of responsibility and accountability", disclosed to owners, authorities, participants and the public. KC3 and KC5 say board and management roles must be clearly specified. KC6 requires a documented board-approved risk-management framework that assigns responsibility for risk decisions and gives risk and internal-control functions authority, independence and resources.
- Principle 8 (Settlement finality): KC1 requires rules that clearly define the point at which settlement is final. KC2 requires final settlement by end of value date at the latest, preferably intraday or real time. KC3 requires a defined point after which instructions may not be revoked. Explanatory note 3.8.1 defines final settlement as "the irrevocable and unconditional transfer of an asset or financial instrument". Nothing on cryptography.
- Principle 15 (General business risk): KC1 requires robust management and control systems for general business risks. KC2-3 require liquid net assets funded by equity, at least six months of operating expenses, plus a recovery or orderly wind-down plan. Nothing on information security.
- Principle 17 (Operational risk): KC1 requires a robust operational risk framework. KC2: the board defines roles and responsibilities and endorses the framework, and systems, policies and controls "should be reviewed, audited, and tested periodically and after significant changes" (the nearest the text comes to change management; the phrase "change management" does not appear in P17). KC5: "comprehensive physical and information security policies that address all potential vulnerabilities and threats". Explanatory note 3.17.12 says information security objectives should conform to commercially reasonable standards for confidentiality, integrity, authentication, authorisation, non-repudiation, availability and auditability. KC6 requires IT recovery within two hours and settlement completed by end of day.
- Cryptography is not named in these principles.

## 2. CPMI-IOSCO, Guidance on cyber resilience for FMIs -- V
- URL read: https://www.bis.org/publications/guidance-cyber-resilience-financial-market-infrastructures.pdf (landing page https://www.bis.org/cpmi/publ/d146.htm).
- Date/version: June 2016 (published 29 June 2016), CPMI Papers 146, final.
- Cryptography and key management: the text extracted contains no mention of key management, "cryptograph*" or quantum. The only relevant wording is in the Protection chapter: protect information "in transit and at rest" with "appropriate encryption (eg, end-to-end encryption)", authentication (for example multifactor) and access control. A later passage lists local storage encryption tools among defences. Extraction was partial, so "no further mention" applies to the extracted text only.
- Testing (Chapter 7): all elements of the framework should be rigorously tested before deployment and regularly after. The programme should be comprehensive, regular and frequent, and informed by cyber threat intelligence. Methods named: vulnerability assessment, scenario-based testing, penetration tests (internet-facing systems regularly and on update or deployment) and red-team tests. Quote: "Testing is an integral component of any cyber resilience framework."

## 3a. DORA, Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 -- V
- URL (official XHTML via Publications Office): https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32022R2554 (EUR-Lex page: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj). OJ L 333, 27.12.2022, p. 1; adopted 14 December 2022.
- Application: Art. 64: enters into force on the twentieth day after publication and "shall apply from 17 January 2025".
- Art. 9(4)(d) (not 9(4) as a whole): financial entities shall "implement policies and protocols for strong authentication mechanisms ... and protection measures of cryptographic keys whereby data is encrypted based on results of approved data classification and ICT risk assessment processes". Art. 9(4)(e) requires documented ICT change management policies, procedures and controls.
- The detailed encryption policy is delegated to the RTS below. Art. 9 itself says nothing on quantum.

## 3b. Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1774 (RTS on ICT risk management framework) -- V
- URL (official XHTML): https://publications.europa.eu/resource/celex/32024R1774 (EUR-Lex: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg_del/2024/1774/oj). Dated 13 March 2024, OJ L 25.6.2024. Art. 42: in force on the twentieth day after publication (calculated: 15 July 2024).
- Art. 6 (Encryption and cryptographic controls): para 1 requires a documented encryption and cryptographic controls policy as part of ICT security policies. Para 2: designed from approved data classification and ICT risk assessment, with rules for encryption at rest and in transit, data in use where necessary, internal and external network traffic, and key management (Art. 7). Para 3: criteria for selecting cryptographic techniques following leading practices and standards (Reg. 1025/2012); entities unable to comply must adopt mitigation and monitoring measures. Para 4: the policy must contain "provisions for updating or changing, where necessary, the cryptographic technology on the basis of developments in cryptanalysis" so it remains resilient (Art. 10(2)(a) DORA); entities unable to update must adopt mitigation and monitoring. Para 5: record and explain such mitigation measures.
- Art. 7 (Cryptographic key management): lifecycle requirements (generate, renew, store, back up, archive, retrieve, transmit, retire, revoke, destroy); controls against loss, unauthorised access, disclosure and modification; methods to replace lost or compromised keys; a register of certificates and certificate-storing devices (at least for ICT assets supporting critical or important functions); prompt certificate renewal before expiry.
- Quantum is not named. The monitoring and change duty is expressed as "developments in cryptanalysis" (Art. 6(4)). Quantum is a reading of that wording, not text.

## 4. EU, A Coordinated Implementation Roadmap for the Transition to PQC (NIS Cooperation Group PQC workstream) -- V
- Landing page: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/coordinated-implementation-roadmap-transition-post-quantum-cryptography (page date 23 June 2025). PDF read: https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/117507, "Part 1, Version 1.1, EU PQC Workstream, 11.06.2025", 17 pp.
- Section 4.1 timeline:
  - By 31.12.2026: all Member States have implemented at least the First Steps; initial national PQC transition roadmaps established; PQC transition planning and pilots for high- and medium-risk use cases initiated.
  - By 31.12.2030: Next Steps implemented; PQC transition for high-risk use cases completed; planning and pilots for medium-risk use cases completed; quantum-safe software and firmware upgrades enabled by default.
  - By 31.12.2035: PQC transition for medium-risk use cases completed; low-risk use cases completed "as much as feasible".
- First Steps (Milestone 1) include cryptographic asset management, dependency maps, quantum risk analysis and supply chain. Next Steps (Milestone 2) include cryptographic agility and a quantum-safe upgrade path. The document recommends that products with expected lifetime beyond 2030 be upgradable to PQC, with post-quantum signatures in the update mechanism.
- Audience is Member States; it is a recommendation, not binding on financial entities.

## 5. NIST IR 8547, Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography Standards (final) -- V (one inference flagged)
- Landing: https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/ir/8547/final (Date Published: October 2026; authors Moody, Perlner, Regenscheid, Robinson, Cooper). PDF read: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ir/2026/NIST.IR.8547.pdf.
- Section 4.1.1 text: SP 800-57 Part 1 had projected disallowing public-key schemes with 112 bits of security on 1 January 2031. NIST "will instead deprecate all quantum-vulnerable digital signature algorithms after 2030 and disallow their use under NIST standards and guidelines after 2035."
- Table 2 (quantum-vulnerable signatures): ECDSA (FIPS 186), EdDSA (FIPS 186) and RSA (FIPS 186) each show "Deprecated after 2030" and "Disallowed after 2035". There is a single row per family, so the final table no longer separates 112-bit (deprecated 2030, disallowed 2035) from 128-bit and above (disallowed 2035 only), as the earlier draft did. Parameter cells use symbol glyphs. Decoded from the PDF font codes, they read ECDSA 112 bits, EdDSA 128 bits, RSA 112 bits, each prefixed by a comparison symbol (probably the greater-or-equal sign). This glyph decoding is my inference; the sentence quoted above applies to all strengths.
- Table 3 lists PQ signatures: ML-DSA-44/65/87 (128/192/256 bits), SLH-DSA, LMS/HSS, XMSS. Finite-field DH and MQV (Table 4) follow the same dates.
- Constraint for US federal high-value assets and high-impact systems: earlier deadlines under EO 14412 and OMB M-26-15 (cited in the document, not read).

- What the search excerpts say (R, unverified): software and firmware signing: support and prefer CNSA 2.0 by 2025, exclusively use by 2030. Traditional networking equipment (VPNs, routers): prefer by 2026, exclusive by 2030. Operating systems: prefer by 2027, exclusive by 2033. Web browsers, servers and cloud services: exclusive by 2033. Niche and custom or legacy equipment: 2030 and 2033. The NSA states the NSS transition to QR algorithms is expected to be complete by 2035, in line with NSM-10. The FAQ reportedly adds that equipment unable to support CNSA 2.0 must be phased out by 31 December 2030 unless excepted, that CNSA 2.0 is mandated by 31 December 2031 and in new NSS acquisitions from 1 January 2027 (the excerpt attributes this to an October 2026 FAQ v2.2).
- The excerpts are internally inconsistent across document versions, so do not cite exact dates from this item without reading the PDFs. I could not read CNSSP 15 at all.
- The EU roadmap (item 4) independently cites the US NSM-10 2035 goal, which supports only the 2035 figure.

## 7. BIS Innovation Hub, Project Leap phase 2: quantum-proofing payment systems -- V for abstract and executive text read; PDF extraction partial
- URL: https://www.bis.org/publ/othp107.htm; PDF https://www.bis.org/publications/project-leap-phase-2-quantum-proofing-payment-systems.pdf (33 pp.). Published 11 December 2025, Innovation Hub project report. Partners: BIS Innovation Hub Eurosystem Centre, Banca d'Italia, Banque de France, Deutsche Bundesbank, Nexi-Colt, Swift.
- System: the Eurosystem's T2 RTGS (access via ESMIG, NSP/DEP message path, HSM-held keys), in a test environment; liquidity transfers. Digital signatures were replaced with post-quantum signatures, which required modifying numerous system components for updated cryptographic libraries.
- Algorithm: CRYSTALS-Dilithium, NIST Round 3 variant (footnote). The report says it was not possible to test ML-DSA (the standardised version), which "will need to be addressed in future testing phases".
- Results: all test scenarios (functional, performance, interoperability) were executed successfully; a message signed with the wrong private key was rejected. Performance: PQ verification was slower "by at least an order of magnitude" than traditional methods (text fragment); the RSA comparison figure is 28.1 ms. The 209.9 ms Dilithium average figure comes only from a secondary website (R) and I did not see it in the extracted text.
- Hybrid: an initial objective was to test hybrid cryptographic implementations, but it was not carried out. The conclusion says the experiment "showed that existing systems need substantial time and resources to develop their ability to accept hybrid cryptographic solutions" (my paraphrase of fragmented text). Whether the cause was a missing certificate in static reference data comes from secondary sources only (R).
- Main conclusions: feasibility of migration shown; learnings on performance, interoperability (surprisingly straightforward) and cryptographic agility; more testing needed; payment systems must become more agile and prepare early with vendor roadmaps.
